THE INFINIAL-FRACTIONATOR

This is a toy to quickly report and analyse repeats in a signal. A signal may represent a structure, and repeats may help reveal it. Here's how it works, and what it produces.

Fractions are the cleanest case. Every fraction, written out in decimals, ends up repeating, and every repeating decimal is a fraction. Find the period and an endless signal folds back into two small numbers:

  1/7 = 0.142857 142857 142857 …
          └─┬──┘
            └─▶ 142857 / 999999 = 1/7
  the period, folded back, is the fraction again.

And for a prime like 7 the period always divides one less than the prime: sevenths repeat in six, never more.

Not every chime is the big bell. At decimal place 762, π rings six nines in a row. Feynman is said to have wanted to learn π up to there, recite "… nine nine nine nine nine nine, and so on", and leave his listeners sure that π was rational. It is not. That is a small bell dressed as the big one, and the Fractionator can tell them apart.

In Indian classical music the beat cycle comes round to sam, its first beat, and the whole room arrives there together. Nobody theorises sam. Everyone waits for it, a reincidence anticipated by all. A period is sam. The Fractionator listens for it, and tells you plainly when it never comes.

Heave in a run of serial signal, and it reports the repeats it noticed. Named by User1, after the White Knight, who would have built it upside down.

A rational number rings the big bell almost at once. An irrational one never does, and never stops ringing the small one.

READING THE PICTURE

  later ─▶
  │╲       ·          the grey diagonal: every moment agrees with itself
  │ ╲   ╲             a short line beside it: a stretch that comes round again
  │  ╲   ╲   ·        evenly spaced lines everywhere: a period, the big bell
  ▼   ╲               dark: nothing recurs there

Across and down are both time. A mark is lit where the stretch at one moment comes round again at another. The commonest little stretches, the words everyone uses and the digit pairs that turn up everywhere, are left out, so what shows is what is notable.

A DOTCOMMA MESSAGE

  .,,,.,,, .,,.,... .,,...., .,,,.,.. ..,..... .,,.,... .,,...., .,,,.,.. .,,.,...
  w        h        a        t        (space)  h        a        t        h
  . is 0 and , is 1, eight marks to a letter.
  Every small letter begins .,, and every space is ..,.....
  So the letters come round as repeats, and the Fractionator hears them.

USE IT YOURSELF

The machine is one file, fractionator.js, and it runs wherever JavaScript does. This page borrows it. So can your page, or a console:

  in a page   <script src="fractionator.js"></script>
              Fractionator.ring(Fractionator.fractionDigits('22/7', 300)).text
  QuickJS     qjs --std fractionator.js --fraction 22/7 --digits 300 --png frac.png
  Node        node fractionator.js --sqrt 2 --digits 20000 --png sqrt2.png
  Python      python fractionator.py --file story.txt --words --png story.png

QuickJS, Node and Python print the same report, word for word, and draw the same picture, pixel for pixel. That was checked, not hoped: eight signals, three engines, every line and every pixel compared. The picture this page shows is made by the same function, and saves as the same PNG.

In the file: ring, period, repeats, recurrence, png, sqrtDigits, fractionDigits, tokens. No libraries, no network, MIT.

LINEAGE

The White Knight, in Through the Looking-Glass (1871), kept his box upside down so the rain couldn't get in, and is generally read as Lewis Carroll's portrait of himself. The Fractionator is his kind of machine: it works forever, it's quite correct, and on an irrational number it never arrives.

The picture is a recurrence plot, a way of seeing repeats that has served physics and signal work for decades. The bells come from a suffix array: every position in the signal, sorted, so that the stretches that share a beginning sit side by side.

Everything runs in this page; nothing is sent anywhere. The machine is fractionator.js; its elder for the command line is fractionator.py. MIT licence.
